# SR-71 engine glow and exhaust

Date: November 3, 2026.

The imported SR-71 now has dedicated recessed engine glow and afterburner volumes. Its imported-aircraft setup bypassed the generic aircraft exhaust path, so the prior generic plume code did not provide this visual effect on the actual SR-71 model.

## Reference direction

Visual references inspected directly:

- [NASA SR-71B takeoff, EC92-1284](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/sr-71-takeoff-with-afterburner/), with a bright nozzle and a visible sequence of warm compression cells.
- [NASA twilight flight, EC93-03092-5, hosted by DVIDS](https://www.dvidshub.net/image/729279/sr-71-flight-with-full-afterburner), for the relationship between the luminous exhaust and the dark airframe.

These guide appearance, not a calibrated spectral-radiance model. Camera exposure, operating point and atmospheric conditions change photographed colors considerably. Reference photos remain local inspection material; no reference photograph is embedded into a game asset.

## Implementation

`src/aircraft/visual/sr71_exhaust.rs` owns the material/plugin, retained geometry, engine-state adapter, glow and lights. Its internal `sr71_exhaust.wgsl` shader integrates an authored emission/density field through a bounded volume.

Each nozzle is centered on the imported mesh, approximately x = ±4.355 m and y = 0.489 m. The volume begins at z = 11.08 m, inside the measured nozzle span of approximately 10.71–11.22 m. The radiant liner is also recessed. Its radius follows the imported nozzle's existing opening scale. These are visual mesh coordinates; this pass does not change propulsion attachment forces or aircraft physics.

Dry power shows a subdued red incandescent liner with softened flame-holder/rib variation. It produces no luminous external flame. Afterburner adds a bright warm core/cells, cooler blue-violet shear-layer color, downstream fade and moving turbulent structure. Cell spacing responds to modeled exhaust Mach and nozzle radius. The field's approximate length grows to about 13 m at full afterburner; this is an appearance parameter, not an independently validated plume-length prediction.

Actual per-engine running mode, powered thrust, visual power and afterburner fuel fraction drive visibility and brightness. One engine can flame out without extinguishing the other effect. Fuel exhaustion/master-off hide the luminous effect. The simulation clock drives motion, so pausing freezes it; the phase wraps continuously without using wall-clock animation. The thermal-looking glow is a visual treatment and adds no solid thermal simulation.

## Bounded rendering work

- Two retained volume meshes and two retained liner draws; the bounding mesh and liner geometry are shared between engines.
- One back-face volume integration per covered pixel. Sample count adapts to the physical ray length through the jet: 12–48 samples, capped at 16 beyond 100 m.
- Two small point lights with shadows disabled, providing warm illumination near the mouths.
- No plume particles, trail history, per-frame mesh generation, transmission-texture copies or compute-fluid simulation.
- Hidden volumes skip uniform preparation. Unchanged transforms, material values and light settings avoid needless updates while paused or inactive.

These are implementation bounds, not a measured FPS improvement. No GPU performance comparison or soak was run for this visual pass.

## Validation

Three focused tests cover independent flameout/shutdown behavior, dry-power visibility conditions and measured nozzle alignment, and retained-entity/world-transform behavior across shutdown and reset. The integration test checks the world-to-volume inverse matrix after rotation/translation and confirms no entity accumulation.

The approved live-app review used **175.67 seconds total**, below the three-minute limit, and captured **12 views across three iterations**. They included daylight side views, night rear views, dry-power nozzle close-ups, full/partial afterburner, one engine off and master-off. Three captures use the final built shader/liner version. Earlier iterations exposed overly thin bright bands and overly strong circular liner markings; the final version softens those shapes, warms the bright cells and adapts integration quality for rearward views.

The native Metal renderer completed the captures without a shader-validation error or application panic. The capture harness restores any sessions it suspended in a `finally` handler. Full logs and images remain under `tests/sr71-exhaust/`; the per-capture executable hashes and cumulative wall time are recorded in `review.json`. Final screenshots and a copy of the record are archived in [sr71-exhaust-evidence](sr71-exhaust-evidence/). Posed captures inspect appearance; they are not flight-handling tests.

Full regression: **882 passed, 0 failed, 1 pre-existing ignored**, with 28.40 seconds test execution. The Mac executable was rebuilt with `cargo build --locked`, copied into the existing `Nord Flight.app`, stripped and ad-hoc signed. Strict/deep signature verification passed.
