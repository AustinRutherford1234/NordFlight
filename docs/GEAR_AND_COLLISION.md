# Landing gear and collision model

G commands a 3.8-second sequence: wheel covers open in 0.65 seconds, legs move over 2.5 seconds, and wheel covers close in 0.65 seconds after either extension or retraction. Separate smaller leg doors follow deployment and remain open around the down struts. `gear_extension` records actual extension independently of the command. Wheels cannot move until their covers are open; commands can reverse mid-cycle. Both mains fold forward and inward into the fuselage, with outward lean delayed until the wheels have cleared the well lips. Wheels rotate flat for stowage. The nose unit has twin wheels, animated steering jacks and a taxi light on its leg door.

The wells are recessed into the airframe; there are no external box housings. Door skins sample the same elliptical stations as the fuselage. Curved skins, liners, stiffeners and hinge pins share rigid parents. Hydraulic door/retraction actuators connect fixed anchors to moving fittings. Two-link drag/breaker braces straighten at deployment and fold for stowage. Oleo pistons and torque links follow measured suspension compression. Forks, axle swivel, rims, fasteners, tread detail and main-wheel brake calipers complete the assemblies. Checks cover tire clearance against skin, liners and ribs throughout deployment and door motion, including fully compressed suspension, and door clearance above level ground.

The main doors use offset gooseneck hinge axes with fixed support arms. This moves the curved rear corners away from the fuselage instead of rotating them through its skin. End edges have a small clearance from the well walls. The fixed wing roots also have actual bay cutouts; they no longer bridge the opening and overlap the moving doors. Open-door envelope and wing-root clipping checks protect these cases.

Reference: [Saab Gripen C cutaway](https://www.saab.com/globalassets/products/aeronautics/gripen-c-series/gripen_c-foldout.pdf), callouts 23–26 and 104, 108–113. It identifies the nose door/taxi light, twin-wheel nose unit, steering jacks, main struts, hydraulic retraction jack, drag/breaker strut and wheel covers that close after cycling. The game's fitted dimensions, pivot locations, angles, linkage curves and timing remain approximations; the reference is an illustration, not engineering CAD or a maintenance manual. Ground support requires at least 98% deployment and healthy gear. Shift commands full throttle; Ctrl commands zero and disengages approach assist.

Contact uses three body-frame wheels at `(0,-1.3,-3.4)` and `(±1.4,-1.3,1.4)` metres. Wheel radius is 0.33 m. The wheel's local vertical speed includes rigid-body angular velocity. For penetration x, normal speed v and travel L=0.32 m, the unilateral normal load is:

`F = clamp(k min(x,L) - c v + 900000 max(x-L,0), 0, 400000)` N.

Main springs use k=170000 N/m and c=16000 Ns/m; nose gear uses k=65000 N/m and c=7000 Ns/m. Each load produces both force and a lever-arm moment. Loads above 220 kN progressively reduce that leg's health. A lower penetration bound protects the integrator from excessive spring penetration. Wheel/strut visuals use the same deployment pose and measured compression; gear parasite drag scales with actual extension. These are tunable game coefficients, not measured Gripen suspension data.

The aircraft airframe uses cached convex pieces, with a circumscribed 16-sided elliptical skin for each tapered fuselage segment and extruded polygons for lifting/control surfaces. A compound shape retains empty space beside the nose instead of using one enclosing box. Hinged collision pieces use the same deflection function as the visual control surfaces. Aircraft tires and struts also participate in structure contact during deployment. The newer lunar spacecraft uses its own four-leg terrain contact and approximate body/capsule clearance checks; see [spaceflight](SPACEFLIGHT.md).

Structure contact uses continuous separating-axis tests against airport boxes, including convex face normals and edge cross axes. Attitude changes are subdivided and given a small conservative angular margin. Terrain checks interpolate both position and attitude and inspect skin vertices over the fixed-step path. This terrain method approximates the smooth analytic terrain; it is not exact triangle-to-terrain continuous collision detection. Tiny fixtures and surface details are represented by the surrounding skin rather than each rendered triangle. F11 draws the active collision skin for inspection.
