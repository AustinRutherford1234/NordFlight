# Flight audio

Flight sounds combine procedural synthesis with an embedded, edited public-domain A-10 cannon recording. No sound packs or runtime network downloads are required. The mix follows the requested cinematic style: strong exhaust roar, distinct missile fly-bys, recorded cannon bursts and substantial explosions. External vacuum stays quiet; a powered spacecraft cockpit carries structural engine vibration and cabin/mechanical sound.

## Controls

- **9** opens/closes the audio panel, including in simplified view.
- **0** mutes/unmutes the mix.
- Master, engines/airframe, weapons/effects and warning levels have separate controls.
- Settings persist in `~/.nord-flight/audio.json`. They do not replace the existing warp/timestep settings. Numeric entry in the warp editor takes priority over these shortcuts.

Defaults are 65% master, 80% engines, 90% effects and 65% warnings. The program does not change the operating system's volume. An optional `NORD_FLIGHT_AUDIO_SETTINGS` file-path override isolates native QA from the player's saved preferences.

## Acoustic model and references

The jet favors broadband turbulent exhaust rather than a steady fan hum. Dry-engine harmonic levels retain their previous 0.08, 0.025 and 0.015 amplitudes. At full afterburner their contribution falls another 80%, and the periodic rumble falls 92%. The mixing-noise layer grows from 1.60 dry to 6.40 wet, with a separate low-frequency pressure layer and irregular, slowly varying swells. The latest mix softens high-frequency hiss: crackle gain falls from 1.80 to 0.35, its input filter coefficient falls from 0.19 to 0.045, and the raw white-noise layer falls to 0.002 at full burner. The bass layer increases from 9 to 12. Burner strength crossfades smoothly. These are source-layer amplitudes before spatial attenuation and output compression, not sound-pressure levels.

Spool controls tonal frequency and the mixing-noise band. To keep dry thrust audible, its source-level curve now rises from 0.16 at idle to 0.78 at the 86% burner threshold, then joins 1.00 at full afterburner continuously. This replaces the steep fourth-power artistic response, which made intermediate throttle settings too quiet. Lighthill's eighth-power acoustic-power scaling informed earlier work, but its low-Mach free-jet assumptions do not describe an entire hot, supersonic fighter exhaust or the current compressed game-volume response.

NASA's [jet-noise spectral-law research](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20120000486) separates mixing and broadband shock noise and discusses operating-condition scaling. Its [high-speed jet acoustics overview](https://nas.nasa.gov/pubs/ams/2021/06-01-21.html) also distinguishes mixing noise, shock-associated noise and screech. The [NASA free-jet measurements](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19740019374/downloads/19740019374.pdf) discuss eighth-power scaling and its applicable limits. These references inform the layers and trends; no measured RM12 spectrum or absolute sound-pressure calibration is used.

The cannon replaces the synthetic buzz with an excerpt from the U.S. Air Force's [A-10 gatling gun strafe](https://www.dvidshub.net/video/593024/10-gatling-gun-strafe), credited to Betty Chevalier / 355th Wing and marked public domain by DVIDS. The first onboard firing pass supplies the 6.2–7.6 second excerpt. Filtering, DC removal, a 25 ms crossfade and normalization produce an embedded 1.375-second loop. Actual rounds refresh a short hold envelope without restarting sample playback; firing stops with a 45 ms release at the end of the hold. Doppler pitch, stereo positioning and cockpit filtering still apply. This intentionally gives the Gripen the requested **A-10 sound style**, rather than claiming BK27 acoustic fidelity. The excerpt includes the recording's cockpit/airframe background noise. Full provenance, processing instructions and source hashes are in [the asset credit](../assets/audio/README.md).

The game's existing firing intervals, ammunition counts and nominal damage remain unchanged. The requested gameplay muzzle velocity remains **4,250 m/s**, five times the original 850 m/s, for both sides. Lead and funnel cues use the same shared speed. The recorded gun's firing cadence is an acoustic effect; it does not spawn additional projectiles.

The player's held firing state sustains the sample on the audio-device clock. This prevents gaps when fixed-step physics runs slower than real time or a frame arrives late; individual simulation shot events no longer determine the player's loop lifetime. Release, ammunition exhaustion, loss of electrical power or aircraft destruction ends sustain and gives a 45 ms release. Player sound identity persists through camera pan/Doppler changes. Enemy gun reports retain event-driven playback and are separate from the player voice.

## State and perspective

- Player and NPC engines respond to running state, spool, afterburner, fuel and failure. Burning missile motors have a separate roar; it fades at burnout. A spacecraft with zero main thrust has no main-engine vibration.
- Stereo bearing and distance attenuation follow the active flight or missile camera. A bounded relative-motion Doppler ratio changes pitch during fly-bys. Exhaust favors observers behind the aircraft. Cockpit attenuation and low-pass filtering retain bass and mechanical vibration while softening the exterior high frequencies. The map reduces engine and effect levels.
- Supersonic continuous sources are audible inside their trailing Mach cone and quiet ahead/outside it. The half-angle follows `asin(1/M)`, using local sound speed and velocity relative to the player's local wind. Aircraft and missile engines use their own velocity, so a subsonic target stays audible while the player is supersonic. Exterior airflow, mechanical layers and the player's sustained cannon also follow the player's cone. A small interior boundary fade and Mach 1.00–1.03 transition prevent abrupt sample discontinuities. Cockpit structural transmission and instrument warnings are exempt. Individual cannon reports, launches and explosions retain propagation from their emission points; they are not blanket-muted based on the player's current Mach number. The geometric rule follows [NASA's Mach-angle explanation](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/BGH/machang.html); it approximates steady straight motion rather than tracking the full history of curved flight and atmospheric refraction.
- Transient atmospheric sounds wait approximately distance/local sound speed in **simulation time**. Own cockpit mechanical events are immediate structural transmission. Delay uses source/listener positions at scheduling; continuous noise uses current geometry. This is a compact game approximation rather than a retarded-time acoustic solver, terrain-occlusion model or calibrated cabin transfer function.
- Aircraft fly-bys produce a synthetic pressure-wave boom when the passing aircraft's analytical Mach cone reaches the listener. The system tracks the player plus all eleven possible NPC aircraft. Other aircraft's booms are audible in the cockpit with 45% cabin gain; your own aircraft's boom is heard only from exterior cameras. The shock has its own longer-range falloff (1.5 km distance scale, 30 km cutoff), rather than the engine's 65 m scale, and fixed waveform pitch. These are cinematic mix parameters, not calibrated pressure levels. Remaining inside produces one boom rather than repeating it every frame. Switching cameras, opening the map, entering the cockpit, resetting, or first crossing Mach 1 does not fabricate a boom. A later exit and re-entry can produce another crossing. NASA explains the observer-dependent behavior in its [science of sonic booms](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2007-august-19-a-sonic-boom/).
- Gear hydraulics follow the actual moving door/leg sequence, followed by a latch clunk. Touchdown adds suspension/tire sound; ground motion adds rolling noise. APU, airflow and fire have separate continuous layers.
- Missile launch/ignition, countermeasure dispensing, hits and breakup have distinct transients. Incoming-missile, stall and dangerous-descent cues take priority over seeker-lock tones. Warning levels are independent of engine/effect volume.
- Pause fades the audible scene and stops delayed-event arrival. Resume preserves pending propagation. Reset/scenario changes discard obsolete pending sounds and mixer voices. High warp keeps normal audio pitch and delivers simulation events at their configured simulation pace.

## Runtime and validation

One Bevy custom stereo source feeds the native audio backend at 48 kHz. The callback has 8 continuous emitter slots and 32 transient voices. Four engine slots represent the player and NPCs; the other four select the loudest burning missiles. Pending propagation is capped at 256 events, with 64-event handoff/game queues. The callback uses a precomputed sine table, fixed voice arrays and nonblocking `try_lock` handoff; it performs no file I/O or per-sample heap allocation. A soft limiter bounds generated samples below digital full scale. Missing audio hardware does not stop flight simulation.

`cargo test --locked` covers relative Doppler signs and attenuation, vacuum/cockpit behavior, propagation and pause, actual gear movement/latch transitions, cannon-event counts/ammunition, bounded queues/voices, nonblocking callback behavior, shutdown, mute/reset, high-warp coast, volume bounds and audio-panel/warp-input interaction.

`cargo run --release --locked -- --study-audio` renders the same mixer offline to `tests/audio-showcase.wav` and writes timing, peak and RMS measurements to `tests/audio-study.json`. The showcase progresses through engine spool/afterburner, cannon fire, gear movement, missile launch/fly-by, explosion, incoming-missile warning, spacecraft cockpit, external vacuum, pause and APU. Its gun cadence matches the game's player cannon interval.

`--visual-check air-audio` runs an unpaused native combat scene, opens the audio panel, saves a screenshot and writes `tests/audio-native-report.json`. The report checks that the native backend is pulling stereo frames and that nonzero PCM reaches it. These checks establish signal generation and backend playback, not subjective listening quality or calibrated real-aircraft fidelity. Current measured results are in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).
